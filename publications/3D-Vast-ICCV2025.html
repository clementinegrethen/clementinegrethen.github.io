Abstract

In this work, we address the challenge of stereo vision for lunar surface reconstruction. We introduce StereoLunar, the first large-scale high-fidelity stereo dataset tailored to the Moon, simulated with ray tracing from real topography and photometric models. Our benchmark evaluates both traditional and deep-learning-based stereo methods in a realistic planetary context. We demonstrate the effectiveness of fine-tuning state-of-the-art models like MASt3R on our dataset, and show substantial improvements over general-purpose pretraining, especially under challenging lunar conditions with low texture and extreme lighting.

Overview

Our dataset is based on DEMs from LRO and uses a physically-based renderer to produce thousands of stereo pairs with dense depth maps, camera poses, and surface normals. The rendering pipeline integrates BRDF models, realistic sun trajectories, and variable noise conditions. We release both synthetic data and a comparison with real lunar landing images.
